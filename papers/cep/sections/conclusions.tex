\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We designed a soft sensor of crystal growth around the camera installed at the sight glass of the Sere\v{d} seed pan
and tested its estimator in simulation and on first real camera points.

\emph{RQ1: what does the installed camera measure, and with what uncertainty?} The log median camera chord equals the log crystal size plus a constant offset
$\theta$, a size-dependent bias and noise. The offset combines a measurement gain, modelled as $3.98 \times 3.65$~\um{}
per PAL sample with an input-uncertainty budget of about $\pm 10$\,\% at 95\,\%, consistent with matched-time tracings of separate crystals (microscope over traced
size 0.85--1.30), with
the selection bias between camera and operator. The in-focus slice is thin enough for one scale. The size-dependent bias comes from resolution at small
sizes, packing late in the strike and the size window.

\emph{RQ2: which growth-state quantities are observable from the camera alone, and what reference makes absolute
size observable?} Under size-independent growth, scaling size and growth rate by a common factor and
shifting $\theta$ leaves the camera signal unchanged. The camera alone observes the relative growth rate, the shape of
the growth curve and the growth-rate ratio between strikes, provided $\theta$ is shared; one microscope sample
restores absolute size and growth age. A size-dependent bias distorts the relative growth rate.

\emph{RQ3: does a camera-aided estimator track the growth state of each strike better and earlier than references
without the camera?} Not yet answered. Classical edge and watershed detectors return medians that stay flat from
before seeding to discharge and fail as a baseline. An extended Kalman filter in
$(\ln L, \ln G, \theta)$, fed a simulated camera signal, lowers the log-size error at held-out microscope times from
0.310--0.319 for the nominal curve to 0.085--0.109 with one anchor, where anchor-only references do no better than the
nominal curve; its scale-free $G/L$ flag catches the late divergence of the two strikes but misses or lags the early
one, and an interim process-signal baseline, driven by whole-massecuite Brix, does worse than the nominal curve.
The simulation tests the filter, not the camera, and it holds only if the camera bias stays constant within a strike: a
packing drift of $\theta$ breaks it. Twelve AI-traced real camera points do no better than the nominal curve where
they exist. The answer waits on the fine-tuned detector and on DCS data for the sampled run
\pending{estimator on the fine-tuned detector signal; process-signal baseline on the DCS export}.

The next steps are the fine-tuned detector validated against hand labels, the DCS export of the sampled run and site
measurements of the camera geometry. The code, configuration and number-generating scripts are released open source
(\ref{app:repro}).
