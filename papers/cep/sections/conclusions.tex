\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We examined what the camera installed at the sight glass of the Sere\v{d} seed pan can measure before any detector
is trusted. The picture is an analogue PAL signal digitised by the recorder; the 1080p clips are screen recordings
of it and add no resolution. A source-classed model of this chain gives a pixel scale of $1.464 \times 1.913$~\um{}
per clip pixel and $3.92 \times 3.59$~\um{} per PAL sample at the focused plane, within about $\pm 10$\,\% at 95\,\%
and dominated by the plant's approximate tube length (RQ1). At F/1.6 the in-focus slice is $\pm 9.4$~\um, and
per-pixel sharpness statistics are sensitive to the recorder overlays and the pillarbox, which must be cropped
(RQ2). In so thin a slice a single focal-plane scale suffices, and a single-component size prior cannot tell front from back: the near/far
labels are tie-breaks (RQ3). The size distributions returned by the edge and watershed detectors follow their area
window and seed constants, so they are not crystal sizes (RQ4).

The seed-pan microscope series of 2026-10-30, with median chords from 29~\um{} to 483~\um{} within two hours of
seeding, defines the range a camera size measurement must cover and the independent reference that a growth-curve
calibration needs. The next steps are a segmentation validated against hand-labelled crystals
(\secref{sec:validation}) and site measurements of the camera model, the tube and the scale at the inner glass face
(\secref{sec:recs}); with both, a camera size signal can enter the growth-state soft sensor of \secref{sec:softsensor}. The
code, configuration and number-generating scripts are released open source (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
