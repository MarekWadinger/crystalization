\section{Growth model and observability \emph{(RQ2)}}\label{sec:growth}

This section gives the growth model the estimator propagates and answers RQ2: which growth quantities the camera
signal of \secref{sec:measurement} determines alone, and what the microscope anchor adds.

\subsection{Growth model}\label{sec:mechanism}

McCabe's $\Delta L$ law states that crystals of all sizes grow at the same rate \citep{McCabe1929,RandolphLarson1988}.
For sucrose, growth per crystal volume has been found size-independent, whereas growth per surface area is linearly
size-dependent \citep{Martins2007}; we use the size-independent form for the median size. The growth rate is driven by
supersaturation \citep{QuintanaHernandez2004}, which the operator changes through the feed, the steam and the
supersaturation target. With the time step $\Delta t$ between camera updates, the growth state of a strike evolves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
L_{k+1} = L_k + G_k \Delta t, \quad \ln G_{k+1} = \ln G_k + \eta_k, \quad \theta_{k+1} = \theta_k,
\end{equation}
with growth rate $G_k > 0$, a random walk in $\ln G$ with process noise $\eta_k$ of variance $Q_G \Delta t$, and the
offset $\theta$, a constant of the installation and the detector ($Q_\theta = 0$). The estimator carries the state in
log coordinates, $x_k = (\ln L_k, \ln G_k, \theta)^\top$, so that the camera output~\eqref{eq:meas} is linear in it.

Across strikes, the seed dose and the supersaturation profile differ. A nominal growth curve $\bar L(\cdot)$ in
physical units, with a time factor $\tau_s$ and a size offset $o_s$ for strike $s$, describes this:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nominal}
L(t; s) = \bar L(\tau_s\, t) + o_s,
\end{equation}
with $t$ the time since seeding. The \emph{growth age} $a$ is the time at which the nominal curve reaches the strike's
size, $\bar L(a) = L(t; s)$; for $o_s = 0$ it is $a = \tau_s t$. It is defined only on the rising part of the nominal
curve. A growth age that falls behind the time since seeding, $\hat a < t$, flags a slow strike, the case in
which the operator would raise the boil-up feed or the supersaturation target. For a linear nominal curve
$\bar L(a) = \bar G a$, with $\bar G$ the nominal growth rate, the strike grows at $G_s = \bar G \tau_s$. The
microscope series shows differences between strikes: at 96~min after seeding the b1 median is 355~\um{} and the b2
median 225~\um{} (\tabref{tab:microscope}). The time since seeding itself is not an estimand: the DCS shows the
seeding (\secref{sec:data}).

A camera cannot be calibrated by assuming a typical crystal size, because the size changes by more than an order of
magnitude within a strike (29 to 483~\um{} in 122~min, \tabref{tab:microscope}). A wrong gain rescales the whole
growth curve without making it internally inconsistent. Lemma~\ref{lem:scale} makes this precise.

\subsection{Observability}\label{sec:observability}

\begin{lemma}[Scale symmetry]\label{lem:scale}
Let one strike follow Eq.~\eqref{eq:growth} for $k = 0, \dots, N$ with any growth-rate sequence $G_k$, observed by the
camera through Eq.~\eqref{eq:meas} with $\beta = 0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every $c > 0$ the state sequences $(L_k, G_k, \theta)$ and $(c L_k, c G_k, \theta - \ln c)$ both satisfy
Eq.~\eqref{eq:growth} and produce the same outputs $y_k$ for every noise sequence. $L_k$, $G_k$ and $\theta$ are
therefore not observable from the camera alone.
\item[(ii)] The relative growth rates $G_k / L_k$, $k < N$, and the shape of the growth curve, $L_k / L_0$, are
invariant under this map and are determined by the noise-free outputs; $G_N$ affects no output. If $G$ is constant
and $\bar L(0) = 0$, so that $L_0 = o_s$, then also $L_k/G = t_k + o_s/G$ is determined.
\item[(iii)] For two strikes $s$ and $s'$ observed with the same $\theta$, the ratio of their growth rates is determined
by the noise-free outputs; for a linear nominal curve this is the ratio $\tau_s / \tau_{s'}$.
\item[(iv)] One noise-free anchor $z_j$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:anchor}) in any strike that shares $\theta$ determines $\theta$,
and with it $L_k$ and $G_k$, $k < N$, in every strike that shares $\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{pf}
(i) Scaling Eq.~\eqref{eq:growth} by $c$ gives $c L_{k+1} = c L_k + c G_k \Delta t$ and adds $\ln c$ to every
$\ln G_k$, and $\ln(c L_k) + \theta - \ln c = \ln L_k + \theta$. (ii) $y_{k+1} - y_k = \ln(L_{k+1}/L_k) = \ln(1 + G_k
\Delta t / L_k)$, so $G_k/L_k = (\exp(y_{k+1} - y_k) - 1)/\Delta t$ for $k < N$ and $L_k/L_0 = \exp(y_k - y_0)$; $G_N$
enters only $L_{N+1}$, which is not observed. With constant $G$, $L_k/G = L_0/G + k\Delta t$, and $L_0 = o_s$ when
$\bar L(0) = 0$. Conversely, two states with equal outputs have equal $\ln L_0 + \theta$ and equal $G_k/L_k$, so they
are related by the map with $c = L_0'/L_0$: the map is the only ambiguity. (iii) With a common $\theta$,
$L_{s,k}/L_{s',k'} = \exp(y_{s,k} - y_{s',k'})$, and $G_s/G_{s'} = (G_s/L_{s,k})(L_{s,k}/L_{s',k'})(L_{s',k'}/G_{s'})$
is a product of determined factors; with $G_s = \bar G \tau_s$ the ratio $\tau_s/\tau_{s'}$ restates it. (iv)
$y_j - z_j = \theta$; then $L_k = \exp(y_k - \theta)$ and $G_k = (L_{k+1} - L_k)/\Delta t$. \hfill$\square$
\end{pf}

\begin{corollary}[Size-dependent bias]\label{cor:beta}
If $\beta(L) = \gamma \ln L$ with a constant $\gamma > -1$, the outputs are $y_k = (1 + \gamma) \ln L_k + \theta + v_k$.
The relative growth rate inferred with $\beta = 0$ is then, for $G_k \Delta t \ll L_k$, $(1 + \gamma)\, G_k/L_k$: biased
by the factor $1 + \gamma$, while the scale symmetry of (i) persists with $\theta - (1 + \gamma) \ln c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{pf}
$y_{k+1} - y_k = (1 + \gamma) \ln(1 + G_k \Delta t / L_k) \approx (1 + \gamma)\, G_k \Delta t / L_k$. \hfill$\square$
\end{pf}

A packing ratio that grows with size, as on the packed frames of \secref{sec:bias}, acts as $\gamma < 0$: the camera
then underestimates the relative growth rate, and the scale-free outputs are no longer free of the detector. This is
why the estimator skips updates outside the valid size range.

The same conclusion holds locally, which is what an extended Kalman filter sees. In the log coordinates of the
estimator the Jacobians are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobians}
\begin{gathered}
F_k = \begin{pmatrix} L_k/L_{k+1} & G_k \Delta t/L_{k+1} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \\
C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad C^z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
for the camera and the anchor. The generator of the map in (i) is the constant direction $q = (1, 1, -1)^\top$: the
first row of $F_k$ sums to one, so $F_k q = q$ at every state, and $C q = 0$. It lies in the kernel of the
observability matrix along every trajectory, so the structural rank condition of \citet{HermannKrener1977} fails in
this direction; an anchor row gives $C^z q = 1$ and removes it.

The log coordinates are a design choice. With $G$ instead of $\ln G$ as state, the direction would be
$(1, G_k, -1)^\top$ and move with the estimate of $G$, and a filter linearised at noisy estimates would gain spurious
information on $\theta$; with $\ln G$ it stays fixed (\code{src/softsensor/ekf.py}, unit test in
\code{tests/test_softsensor.py}).

A numerical check confirms the structural result and shows how well the observable part is determined in practice
(\code{scripts/softsensor_results.py}, SIMULATED). With a noise-free camera every minute for 60~min from
$L_0 = 40$~\um{} at $G = 4$~\um/min, the camera alone gives rank 2 with null vector $(1, 1, -1)$, and one anchor at
30~min gives rank 3. In a filter run over b1 with diffuse priors on $\ln L$ and $\ln G$, the standard deviation of
$\theta$ stays at its prior of 0.160 without an anchor and falls to 0.0876 with one. \tabref{tab:ident} gives the
practical identifiability of the structurally observable $\ln(G/L)$ under the evaluation priors of
\secref{sec:ekf}: a standard deviation of 0.45 at 60~min in b1 is a factor of about 1.5 at one sigma, and it widens
again when the medians stop growing. The anchor changes these values by at most 0.025, because it informs only the
scale direction.

The fusion of fast online outputs with slow laboratory samples follows the multirate estimators of \citet{Gudi1995}
and \citet{FatehiHuang2017}; augmenting a filter with a constant bias state follows \citet{Friedland1969}, here as a
multiplicative gain that becomes additive in the log. The scale symmetry and its corollary are our derivation.

Three consequences shape the estimator. First, without an anchor the uncertainty of the absolute size never falls
below the prior uncertainty of $\theta$, which the gain budget and the selection bias set together. Second, what the
camera alone delivers, the relative growth rate, the curve shape and the comparison between strikes, is what the
operator needs to see that a strike grows slowly. Third, (iv) holds only if $\theta$ is shared: the same camera,
optics and detector, and a selection bias that does not change with packing. Whether one anchor per run suffices is
therefore an empirical question (\secref{sec:validation}). A growth-rate model driven by process signals inherits the
same symmetry unless its kinetic parameters are known in physical units, so the process-signal baseline also needs the
microscope anchors. \secref{sec:estimator} builds the filter on these results.
