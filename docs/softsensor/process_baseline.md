## 5. What changes when the 2026-10-30 DCS export arrives

The requested export (QI1003, TI1006, EI1004, PIC1001, PIC1002_PID OUT,
PIC1003_PID, QIC1000/1001, TIC1002, and LI1000 if it exists) replaces three
inputs of §2 and nothing else:

1. **Driver.** Bx(t) = QI1003 at 30 s instead of 7–8 lab Brix per
   strike interpolated linearly; T(t) = TI1006 instead of the ASSUMED
   70.8 °C. The temperature sweep of §3 is then dropped. Purity stays
   the lab purity interpolated between samples (no online purity exists);
   the recipe value 93.4 % is the fallback.
2. **Seeding time.** t = 0 moves from the sidecar operator times (b1
   11:29:33, b2 15:22:00) to the steam cut found by
   `process.detect_seeding` (on 2026-02-04 the cut was 1.5 min after the operator's time). Every microscope sample time
   since seeding shifts by that difference; the estimator and the
   baseline both use the DCS time.
3. **k_g** is refitted with the DCS driver, b1 → b2 and b2 → b1, and the
   rows of §2 are recomputed with the same metrics, held-out samples and
   anchor draws. The row label changes from "process-signal baseline
   (interim, lab-sample driver)" to "process-signal baseline (DCS)" and it
   replaces the interim row in tab:loso.

The export also gives the first measured comparison of QI1003 with the lab
Brix at the 7–8 lab samples per strike (both whole-massecuite readings; an
offset there moves KP directly). It does not remove the main limitation:
neither signal is the mother-liquor Brix after the first minutes, so KP
stays an index. A mother-liquor measurement (a centrifuged or filtered lab
sample) or a crystal-content estimate would be needed to turn it into a
supersaturation. Unchanged: the growth law, g, L(0), the metrics and the
n = 2 strikes.

Code path: `_dcs_rows()` and §4 already run the DCS driver on
`Paulen_data.xlsx`; for 2026-10-30 it needs the export path, the same tag
names, and the lab purity interpolation of `lab_driver` in place of the
constant 93.4 %.

## 6. Limitations

- **Whole-massecuite Brix.** Lab Brix of a diluted massecuite sample and QI1003 both include the crystals; the plant formula wants the mother liquor. KP from them overstates the liquor supersaturation as crystal content grows (KP 1.04 → 2.27 over the post-seeding b1 lab samples at 70.8 °C), so the fitted G = k_g (KP − 1)^g rises over the strike where the microscope growth rate between samples is 3.5–4.9 µm/min from 16 to 122 min in b1, then stops in tightening. k_g is a curve-fit constant of this index, not a kinetic constant. The diagnostic g < 1 rows of §3 fit better for that reason; they are not a literature growth law.
- **ASSUMED temperature** on 2026-10-30 (no DCS); §3 shows how much the result moves over 68–74 °C.
- **n = 2 strikes**, one sampled run: one fit per direction, no estimate of between-strike or between-day variability. The interim driver has 7–8 lab points per strike, linearly interpolated.
- **No tightening or discharge model**: L keeps growing through tightening (between the 'pred sušením' and 'po sušení' samples, b1-s12, b2-s09 being the latter), where the microscope medians plateau, fall or jump.
- **Seed size**: L(0) = 13 µm from 4 chords on one slurry photo, the same for both strikes; b1's first post-seeding median is already 29 µm at 2.5 min (crystals before seeding: residual footing or nucleation, open), which a process model cannot know without a size measurement.
- **Purity**: lab purity of the whole massecuite (crystals raise it); 2026-02-04 uses the recipe value 93.4 % (ASSUMED).
- Microscope truth: one AI transcription of operator-chosen crystals (as in `results.md`).
- SC form: the plant sheet (2.72·(−c·NW)) reproduces the DCS live KP (unit test); the textbook exp form lowers KP by 6–9 % on the lab points and is shown in §3.
- Lohani et al. (2026, ESCAPE 36), our group's supersaturation soft sensor from standard process measurements, was not found as code in this repo, its sibling worktrees or the Drive `sugar_plant` folder, so it is not reused here; this baseline uses the plant's own KP formula instead.
