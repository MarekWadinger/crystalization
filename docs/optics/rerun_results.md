about 8 minutes. Paper sources: `papers/cep/main.tex` and `papers/cep/sections/*.tex`.

Columns: **Old** = the value in the paper now. **New** = the value recomputed now. **Reproduced** =
the leading numbers of *new* match *old* to ±1 in its last digit. **Scale**: `x` or `y` = a length
along a row or a column, `area` = area-equivalent (geometric-mean pixel), `free` = no pixel scale
enters. **Weakest class** = weakest source class among the config inputs (`SOURCE_RANK` in
`optics.config`).

No hand-labelled crystal annotations exist yet. Nothing below is a crystal-size measurement: the
detector CSD rows are labelled as detector artefacts.

## What changed, in short

1. **Scale.** The 1080p clip pixel is **1.464 µm (x) × 1.913 µm (y)** in the slurry at focus
   (95 %: ±≈ 10 %, ESTIMATE), not an isotropic 1.66 µm. The geometric mean is 1.673 µm, so
   *area-equivalent* sizes on the clips move by only +0.8 %. Lengths along a row shrink by 12 %,
   lengths down a column grow by 14 %. The PAL sample is 3.92 × 3.59 µm.
2. **What "one pixel" means for the depth of field.** The resolution element is the CCD cell / PAL
   sample (6.83 × 6.25 µm at the sensor, geometric mean 6.53 µm). A 1080p clip pixel (2.92 µm
   geometric mean) is an upscaled PAL sample, so it is not a resolution limit. The paper's DoF
   numbers reproduce only with the clip pixel, which by coincidence is close to the old 2.9 µm. With the
   PAL sample, which `optics.plots` now draws (figs 03, 04):

   | Quantity | Paper | 1 clip px (2.92 µm) | 1 PAL sample (6.53 µm) |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | in-focus half-slice, F/1.6 | ±4.2 µm | ±4.2 µm | **±9.4 µm** |
   | in-focus half-slice, F/16 | ±42 µm | ±42 µm | **±94 µm** |
   | sharpness FWHM, F/1.6 | 4.9 µm | 4.5 µm | **10.5 µm** |
   | δ at CoC = 5 px | ±21 µm | ±21 µm | **±47 µm** |
   | near/far size discrimination there | 0.08 % | 0.08 % | **0.17 %** |
   | ±0.2 mm overlay window / half-slice | 48× | 47× | **21×** |

   `optics.disambiguate.fig_discriminability` (fig 10) used the clip pixel; since the fix pass of 2026-11-03 it
   draws the PAL sample (`resolution_pitch_um`). Before that, the code used
   both definitions and the paper uses one.
3. **α is scale-free and reproduces**: 5.36 (no crop), 4.57 (DVR overlay only), 4.88 (`DEFAULT_CROP`,
   −2.5 %). The auto crop that `optics.measure` actually runs (`resolve_crop`, 1826 × 906 px)
   gives **4.90 (−2.0 %)**. That is the value fig 08 shows.
4. **Frame 750 counts are not reproduced by any configuration.** Fig 11 (`optics.qualitative`, current
   window) now draws **840** edge detections (paper: 55); the legacy window gives 292. Fig 17
   (`optics.detect_watershed`, `LayerConfig()` defaults 50–50 000 px², aspect 4) shows **441 / 981**
   (paper: 102 / 740, "14 %" → 45 %). The committed fig 17 already showed 441 / 981 before this rerun,
   so the paper text was out of date with its own figure.
5. **CSD (detector artefacts).** With the legacy `[80, 8000]` px² window, both §7 tables reproduce to
   the last digit (edge 18 823 / median 0.037 / CV 0.83 / s 0.57 / 70:30; watershed 52 302 / 0.075 /
   0.40 / 0.40 / 72:28; the +0.001 mm shifts are the 1.673 vs 1.66 geometric mean). With the
   current window `[9, 356 474]` px²:
   - the **watershed barely moves**: 52 784 detections, median 0.075 mm, CV 0.43, s 0.41, 76:24. Its
     size distribution follows its own seed spacing (`_PEAK_MIN_DISTANCE` = 12 px = 17.6 × 23.0 µm);
   - the **edge detector follows the window**: 47 414 detections, median 0.018 mm, CV 2.16, s 0.80,
     80:20.

   Neither run is a crystal-size measurement.
6. **Bayes near/far is a tie-break.** In all four runs, 99.6–99.9 % of the off-focus detections have
   p_near within 0.500 ± 0.002 (0.1–99.9 % quantiles 0.497–0.504). The 70–80 % "near" splits come
   from `side = "near" if p_near > 0.5`, not from evidence. The mean depth correction is ≤ 0.013 % of
   the median.
7. **Unsourced numbers.** The dent analysis (Laplacian² medians 11 664 / 12 996, c = 5.8 µm, fig 17
   dents) and the 42.5 px autocorrelation have no script in the repo. Only the algebra downstream of
   them is recomputed: 55.074 mm, δ = 8.4 µm, H → front 21.5 mm, H′ 12.0 mm. At µm_x the 85 px
   texture period is 0.125 mm, not 0.14 mm.
8. **Other stale numbers.** `tests/test_optics.py` has 19 tests (17 before the fix pass), not 10. The e = 10 → 60 mm revision
   is 5.5× in µm/px (6.0× in e), not "4.5×". At e = 10 mm the clip pixel would be 8.12 × 10.61 µm,
   not 10.4.

## Code issues found (not fixed here: `src/` is out of scope)

1. `src/scale_overlay/render_demos.py:85` builds `lens_from_config(cfg)` without a frame size (a PAL
   sample) and draws it on the 1920 × 1080 `frame_0.png`, so its grid is 2.3× too coarse. Also,
   `src/scale_overlay/overlay.py` draws only square grids. Fix: `lens_from_config_and_video`-style frame
   size plus per-axis spacing, or the resampling used in `--fig07`.
2. `src/optics/detect_watershed.py:319` uses `LayerConfig()` defaults instead of
   `detection_area_px`, and `DEFAULT_CROP` instead of `resolve_crop`. Fig 17 therefore uses a third
   window, different from figs 11 and 14.
3. Crops are inconsistent: `optics.qualitative` and `render_demos` use `DEFAULT_CROP`; `measure`,
   `disambiguate`, `csd` and `kpi` use the auto crop (1826 × 906).
4. (Fig 10 fixed in the fix pass; σ_floor still the clip pixel, effect stated in sizing.tex.) "One pixel" is defined two ways: `optics.plots` (figs 01–04, 06) uses the PAL sample; `fig_discriminability`
   and `disambiguate`'s σ_floor use the clip pixel (2.92 µm).
5. (Fixed in the fix pass, except the tube sweep.) `fig_discriminability` hard-codes δ ∈ [0.1, 50] mm. `fig_measured_vs_theory` draws its fit line
   off the fitted band. `fig_coc_vs_distance` has overlapping text. `fig_extension_tube_sweep` ignores
   `focus_travel_mm`.
6. `optics.config.um_per_px_xy_at_focus` uses the rounded `[focus].object_distance_mm` = 55.1:
   1.4654 vs `scale_chain` 1.4644 µm/px (0.07 %).
7. Stale comments: `psf.sharpness_gaussian` ("1.4 µm = pixel_pitch/2 for the Hanwha HCB-6000"),
   `detect_watershed` header ("~1.7 um/px", "≈ 0.040 mm at 1.66 µm/px"), `render_demos` docstring
   ("±6 µm"), and the `pipeline_diagram` Stage F box.
9. (Fixed in the fix pass.) `optics.psf.sharpness_diffraction` used the nominal F-number; at M = 1.743 the
   image-side working F-number is N(1 + M) = 2.74 N. It now takes `magnification`; rows 106–109 at the end
   of the table give the FWHM (F/1.6 0.37, F/8 1.83, F/16 3.65 PAL samples) and F/4.4 as the one-sample limit.
   The F/1.6 PAL-floor FWHM stays 10.5 µm; the clip-pixel FWHM moves 4.5 → 5.5 µm.
8. `tests/test_crystal_rules.py::test_frame_scale_converts_her_pixels_for_pal_and_1080p` fails (1.195
   vs 1.42) after `bfa6295`, as the scale work reported.
